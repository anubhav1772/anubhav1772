My areas of interest include robotics, reinforcement learning, and learning-based control, with a particular focus on legged robotic systems, including quadrupeds and humanoids. My research has primarily focused on energy-efficient locomotion, adaptive gait control, fall recovery, and robust sim-to-real transfer. I have experience with physics simulation environments such as Isaac Gym, MuJoCo, and PyBullet, along with ROS 2 for robotic software development, simulation, and system integration. I am currently expanding my simulation and learning-based control workflows toward NVIDIA Isaac Lab and Isaac Sim, particularly for humanoid robotics and sim-to-real applications.

My broader research interests include <b>model-based reinforcement learning, learned world models, uncertainty-aware control, imitation learning, and generative action policies</b>, including diffusion-based and flow-matching approaches. I am also exploring <b>Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and multimodal foundation models</b> for intelligent robotic systems.
I am particularly interested in developing predictive and adaptive control frameworks that leverage learned dynamics and uncertainty estimation to anticipate instability, detect unfamiliar operating conditions, and enable proactive adaptation or recovery. I also aim to investigate how world models, generative action policies, and multimodal foundation models can improve generalization, decision-making, and autonomous behavior in legged and humanoid robots operating in complex, dynamic environments.

Outside my primary research, I enjoy working on independent robotics projects that involve developing end-to-end autonomous systems. These projects allow me to explore the integration of learned control policies with perception, state estimation, planning, and navigation using frameworks such as ROS 2. My goal is to bridge the gap between learning-based algorithms and practical robotic applications, gaining hands-on experience with complete robotic software pipelines.
